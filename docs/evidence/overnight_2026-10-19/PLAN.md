## How the night runs

The failure mode to avoid is the loop: a lane rerunning a suite, a gate rerun to move blame, a third attempt at a task, an idle agent waiting. Every number below exists to make a loop impossible.

**Seats.** Opus: lanes 1, 3, 5, 6, 9, 14, 15, 16, 17, 20, 22, 26, 27, and the reviews. Sonnet: the four deletion lanes, both coupling censuses, lanes 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 18, 19, 21, 23, 24, 25. Haiku: given commands only, for example the stills capture and hash compare for lane 3, a gate run the orchestrator names, a `bd show` sweep for lane 12. Never Haiku for an edit or a judgment. Every spawn names its model and effort; effort is medium except Opus lanes at high.

**Concurrency.** At most three lanes live, at most two of them building. Read-only lanes (coupling censuses) do not count against the build cap. The orchestrator keeps the sequencing above as its queue and starts the next lane only when a slot and a crate are free. Nothing is spawned ahead of need.

**Tests, per lane, once.** A lane runs each check once to the end, fixes every failure it shows, reruns only those failures by name, and never runs the full set again. Never `--workspace`, never a whole crate's suite, never a rerun of a green.

**Scope, so nothing unrelated runs.** UI flows run only by name (`scripts/run_ui_flows.py <flow-name>`; its filters match by substring, so name the flow, not a word) or through the gate's own `--touched` mapping, which picks flows per touched file from `scripts/ui-flows/manifest.json` (at most 14 for the heaviest file, usually under 5). A bare `run_ui_flows.py` with no filter runs all 127 and is never typed tonight. Nextest runs by filterset for the touched modules, never `-p <crate>` alone. A headless render is one render of the named surface for the named check, never a sweep. Every brief lists the flows and filtersets its lane may run; a lane that wants to run something not on its list writes a bead instead. The gate's mapped set runs once per batch, and a deletion lane that touched many files accepts the larger mapped set once rather than running pieces of it along the way.
- Deletion lanes: `cargo check -p <crate>` after each batch of rows; clippy `--tests` on the touched crates once at the end; then `scripts/landing_gate.py --repo "$WT" --cheap` once. Its passes are reused at landing.
- Lane 1: each red by name only, never its suite; the cheap gate once at the end.
- Lane 3: `cargo check -p manifold-nodes-scene` per pass; clippy and the scene crate's tests once after the last pass; stills captured once before the first pass and once after the last. If the stills differ, bisect by pass, not by rerunning everything.
- Lane 5: the cure-test by name, then the touched modules' tests once, then the cheap gate once.
- Lanes 6, 7, 8, 14, 15, 16, 17: clippy on the touched crates, their tests once, the named UI flows or renders once, the cheap gate once.
- Lane 9: per commit the touched surface's flows with `AssertFresh` and one headless render; the cheap gate once at the end of the lane.
- Lanes 18, 19: one render or flow per fix, the cheap gate once at the end.
- Lane 10: `test_preToolUseBash.py` and `test_landing_gate.py` by path, once. Lane 11: the one test by name. Lanes 12, 13: `design_status.py`, the docs-index freshness test and the two prose guards by name.
- Loop breaker: three reruns of the same failing test is the cap. Still red after three: `expected_fail` with a bead, and the lane carries on with its remaining rows. A task that has failed twice (the lane and one respawn) is parked with a bead and the orchestrator moves to the next; there is no third attempt at the same task, but the night continues.

**Gates and landing, in batches.** The landing gate is the expensive thing, so lanes land in batches with one gate each, their cheap passes reused. Batch 1: green main, the data and edit bugs, plus the engine-model and engine-runtime deletions (needs GPU, for the proof reds). Batch 2: graph and app-ui deletions, the per-frame waste lane, the switched-off features lane, the old-format loading lane, plus the process lane, with `--skip-gpu`. Batch 3: param lookup, the two playback bugs, effect ids, typing and the repaint conversion (the roots). Batch 4: draw by kind, gates, the sky sun and the render_scene split (the trunk; needs GPU, for the stills and light renders). Batch 5: colour row, the two-pane Scene tab, materials, the small fixes, audio defaults and the ratchet (the leaves; renders, GPU as the gate's mapping asks). Lanes 12 and 13 land direct, docs and beads only. A gate that fails is diagnosed from its output, not rerun; blame that moves between two runs means the measurement is wrong, so park that batch with a bead and carry on with the others. All GPU work through `scripts/gpu_queue.py`.

**Reviews.** One Opus adversarial review per batch, five all night. Deletion diffs are all red and pure-move diffs are all moves, so the orchestrator skims those itself from `git diff --stat` and `--color-moved`; the Opus reviewer reads every fix and convergence in full. Nothing lands past a review that was not read.

**Token discipline.** Each brief carries the file:lines and commands from this plan so the lane re-derives nothing. The orchestrator reads reports and diff stats, never whole diffs or files; the reviewer reads diffs. A lane that has reported is stopped at once. Lanes run builds in the foreground with a timeout and never go idle with a build running; the orchestrator follows gates with `scripts/watch_land.py`, nothing else waits. No polling, no sleep loops. No new scripts, probes or tools, however convenient; the lane that wants one has misread the task. Anything a lane finds outside its brief becomes a bead, not a detour.

**Rough budget, as a signal only.** About 32 lane spawns, thirteen of them Opus, five Opus reviews, five gates, about ten GPU runs, two to three lanes live at any time. Running past that is a reason to look for a loop (a suite rerun, a gate rerun, a second respawn), never a reason to stop. If no loop is found, the night keeps going.

**Continuation queue, when the lanes are done.** In this order, same rules: the grade-A rows of the rescreen, tooling and patterns sweeps at 0291dac8f, each sweep as its own Sonnet lane (rescreen-011 waits: its review ties it to water-009, which the water review keeps).
